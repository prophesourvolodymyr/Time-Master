## Between sets: what studies show (all small, acute, mostly reps to failure)
| Study | Who and what | Between-set work | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arazi 2015, Facta Univ Phys Educ Sport 13(3):329-339 [PR] | 18 male bodybuilders, bench and leg press, 4 sets, rests of 3 and 5 min | dynamic vs static vs none | no significant difference in reps; "the rest interval is more important than the type of rest" |
| Nasiri 2011, quoted in Iida 2025, not opened | bench press | 50 s dynamic stretch, 4 muscle groups | more reps than passive rest |
| Iida 2025, J Hum Sport Exerc 20(1):328-337 [PR] | 12 men, randomized crossover, 4 sets of 3 vertical jumps, 240 s rests | dynamic stretch before and between sets, only before, or none | jump height higher in the later sets with between-set stretching; with stretching only before, set 4 was lower than set 1 (p = .001) |
| Miranda 2015, Res Sports Med 23(1):37-50 [PR] | 10 trained men, seated row to failure, 2 min rests | 40 s static stretch of the antagonist (chest) | more reps and more lat and biceps activity than passive rest |
| Int J Exerc Sci 2022;15(4):231 [PR] | 17 resistance-trained men, 7 sets of cable fly | 45 s static stretch of the working muscle | lower total load, less swelling and lactate than passive rest; 1RM change the same; authors suggest lower-intensity days |
| Front Sports Act Living 2025;6:1483972 [PR] | 14 untrained men, leg extension at 70% | 20 s static stretch, or walking | no difference in reps or volume load vs passive rest |
| J Strength Cond Res 2019, systematic review of interset strategies (abstract only) | mixed | many | "dynamic agonist/static antagonist stretching, cooling, aerobic exercise, vibration and individualized heart rate-based intervals seem to be the most effective"; study designs differ a lot |

Reading:
- Dynamic or other-muscle stretching between sets did no harm in these studies and raised reps or jump height in some (n = 10-18). Static stretching of the working muscle for 45 s lowered the work done in one study.
- [INF] The jump study is the closest to sprints and plyos. Nothing tests planche, front lever or handstand holds, or fight rounds.
- [INF] Rest length matters more than what is done in it (Arazi): keep rests full and fit the easy moves inside them.

## Deep stretching in the morning
- Static stretching right before maximal work: under 30 s no harm (pooled -1.1%), 30-45 s about -1.9%, 60 s or more a significant drop (Kay and Blazevich 2012, as checked in camp-research-audit.md row 9).
- Flexibility by time of day: thin. Edwards (n = 16): whole-body flexibility 3.0 cm better later in the day, goniometry "no detectable effect". Power peaks about 13:00-20:00 and is lowest in the morning (Knaier 2022: effect sizes 0.73 Wingate, 0.79 jump; bias risk moderately high). See audit row 11.
- User decision: deep stretches only after training or in the evening; mornings get active mobility.
