# Planche, front lever, muscle-up beside a fight camp: evidence notes (2026-11-17)

Tags: **PR** peer-reviewed (design, n in source list). **P** practitioner (named coach). **L** lore/anecdote. **I** my inference. "S#" = source list at the bottom.

## 0. Bottom line
- **No peer-reviewed dose study for planche or front lever turned up.** A Europe PMC title/abstract search for these skills gave 10 hits, none on dose: a muscle-up EMG study (S12), a planche simulation (S17), a ring EMG paper I did not use, two lat-tear case reports (S13-14). Abstract searches miss full-text mentions (S1 names the skills only in its full text). Dose advice is coach consensus; every coach below sells something. [PR absence]
- Tendon is the limiter, and its data are lower-limb only (S2-S3). In 8 weeks he gains muscle/neural strength first, tendon lags: that is the injury window. [I]
- Injury odds rose with vigorous activity and prior injury (S1, see 1d); a fight camp adds vigorous load. [PR, I]
- Strength gains track weekly volume and intensity, not frequency (S9). Maintaining strength needs intensity, not volume (S5).
- Challenge to his plan: coaches want 2-3+ exposures per skill per week to progress (1b). Three new skills "not many times a week" means maintenance-level progress at best, and any quick planche step-up in 8 weeks would be muscle-led, ahead of the tendon. [P, I]
- His level: front lever closest, bar muscle-up next, planche furthest. [I]

## 1. Planche and front lever

**1a. Peer-reviewed.** Nothing on holds, sets or frequency. Planche appears only as an 80-body-type simulation: body type sets innate ability (S17). One ring static-element training study, n=8 elite gymnasts (S18). Tendon (S2, PR meta-analysis, 27 studies, Achilles/patellar only): stiffness rises with high intensity (>70% of max); 12+ week programmes trended better, not significantly; S4 (PR systematic review, abstract) agrees. About 3 s of strain per cycle works; 12 s added nothing (S3, PR narrative). In two 3-month studies strength led tendon stiffness by 1-2 months; tendon collagen half-life is ~10x muscle protein (S3). No biceps, elbow or wrist time course exists. [PR]

**1b. Coach dose (commercial or semi-commercial).**
| Coach | Hold / sets | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Steven Low, DPT (S19-S21) P | Working hold = 60-70% of max hold (max 10 s: 6x7 s; max 20 s: 4-5x13 s). Advanced planche day: 4-5x6-10 s holds, 3-5 sets pseudo-planche push-ups, 3x25 scapular work | 2-3 full-body days or 2-day split (e.g. straight-arm/bent-arm); holds are strength, not "skill" |
| GMB, Ryan Hurst (S22-S23) P | Work up to 5x20 s at the current step, rest 2-3 min; test next step 3-5 s x up to 8; then 5x10 s back-off | Planche max 3 days/wk; 4th day after 2 months; daily with intensity waves only after 4-5 months; rotate planche with pulling |
| Sommer, GymnasticBodies (S26-S27) P | Fixed load 8-12 weeks, never raise load and volume together | Foundations: 20-min sessions, 2-3 h/week (second-hand) |
| FitnessFAQs, Daniel Vadnal, physio (S28) P | not stated | Planche Pro 4 push days x 1.5 h; Lever Pro 3x/wk x ~1 h |
| Tom Merrick (S35) P, description only | 10-12 s holds when max is 15 s; planche and lever alternated, 20 min | one session |
| Get Gymnast Fit, "Trainer Maxim" (S30) P | chapter lists only: straight-arm presses, lean holds, tuck hold, L-sit to straddle planche; no numbers readable | his courses put a rest/deload week at week 5 |
| Anton, Calisthenicmovement (S29, S31) P | nothing numeric readable; Anton's lever chapters name "volume vs intensity" and "autoregulation" | not stated |

Overlap: working holds 6-20 s, 4-6 sets, 2-3 min rest, 2-3 sessions/week, stop when form breaks. [I]

**1c. Connective-tissue timeline.** Sommer, in his words (S26): muscle turns over in ~90 days, connective tissue 200-210 days, "6 or 7 months of dial it back"; unsourced, same direction as S3. Notes of his podcasts: 4-6 years for ring planche or cross (S27, second-hand). GMB's rule implies 2 and 4-5 months (S22). Bracket: months before dose limits loosen, years for advanced levels. [P, I]

**1d. Injury patterns, warning signs.** S1 (PR, case-control level 4, survey, n=93): 62.4% injured in 12 months; tendinopathy 31.0% of the injured; shoulder 23.0%, upper/mid back 18.4%, then elbow, wrist; at injury they were doing muscle-ups 12.1%, planche 12.1%, front lever 10.3%; "overtraining" the top perceived cause (56.9%); prior injury OR 4.1, top tertile of vigorous activity OR 5.6; ≥3 skill sessions/week OR 7.3 (CI 1.2-43; univariate; reference group of 7). Berg with "Dr Yaad" (S32, P): forearm splints after planche/handstand (wrist hyperextension); golfer's elbow from false-grip holds, muscle-up transitions, pulling ("A: stubborn"); triceps tendinopathy from planche/HSPU; distal biceps tear after an unfamiliar grip ("often no warning"), proximal tear after years of shoulder pain. Rules: joint or connective-tissue soreness is the start of overuse, deload then ramp slowly (Low, S21, P); with wrist pain keep it ≤2-3/10 and not worse next day (S32); any shoulder pain, drop a level, persistent pain see a doctor (GMB, S23); do not raise intensity and volume together (Sommer, S27).

**1e. Staging.** Front lever: tuck, advanced/open tuck, one-leg, straddle, half-lay, full. GMB trains tuck, open tuck, straddle, full, each as negative then pause (S23); Anton's chapters add one-leg, half-lay (S29). Planche: lean, frog/crane, tuck, advanced tuck, one-leg/straddle, full; after tuck GMB calls the order fluid: pick two variations for ≥3 weeks (S22). Most people stall at advanced tuck, one-leg or straddle (Low, S19). Time: GMB "under 6 months to 2 years" for a planche (S22). [P]

## 2. Muscle-up
| Source | Pull | Dip | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| CrossFit, Matthieu Dubreucq (S34) P | 15 (Glassman 2002), "nobody really knows" | 15 | chest-to-ring pull-up with false grip; "can take years" |
| GMB (S24-S25) P | 5 snappy full-range | 5 good | transition is the gate; many with a dozen of each fail |
| Berg Movement, John Donohue (S33) P | high pull: chest, lower ribs, navel to bar | deep ring dip | over-grip (half false grip) |
| Sommer (S26) P | n/a | n/a | skip muscle-ups in first 6 months: weak shoulder extension traps elbows |

- **What builds it:** CrossFit: weekly negatives (5-7 singles) for pull and dip, strict sets, 2-3 x 10-15 min transition drills, 1-3 attempts/week, banded on low rings (S34). GMB: feet-down "baby" muscle-up, not bands (S24). Berg: kip early, then strip it (S33). Chapter lists only: Anton (explosive pull-ups, banded muscle-ups; S29), Get Gymnast Fit (support holds, bounce-offs, jumps, bend-assisted; S30). [P]
- **Frequency/time:** GMB 2-4 sessions x 30-60 min, six weeks, no guarantee (S25); FitnessFAQs 3x60 min, first 8-week block "unlocks" the first rep (S28, marketing). I found no published time-to-first-muscle-up. With 12-15 strict pull-ups: weeks to a few months for a bar rep, longer on rings. [I]
- **Injury:** rings demand more biceps, forearm flexor, upper trap and triceps work than bar; learn bar first (S12, PR EMG, n=10). Two single-patient lat tears during muscle-ups (S13-S14, PR case reports). GMB: false-grip wrist pain, load the palm pad; kipping risks overuse or trauma (S24, P).

## 3. Concurrent skills with a combat sport
**Shared tissue** [I, from S12 muscle maps, coach statements, injury data]:
| Tissue | HS | Planche | FL | MU | Boxing | Wrestling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wrist/forearm | high | high | grip | false grip | high | grip |
| Anterior shoulder | high | high | end range | transition | high (S36) | high (S16) |
| Biceps tendon, brachialis, forearm flexors | - | high | high | high | - | high |
| Lats | - | - | high | high (S13-14) | - | high |

Amateur boxing: training injuries are mostly upper-limb sprains/strains (S15, PR, 17 studies). Wrestling: shoulder 14.1%, elbow 7.2% of injuries (S16, PR, level 4).

- **Most conflict:** planche + handstand (wrists, anterior shoulder; S32 ties forearm pain to both) and FL + muscle-up + pull-ups + wrestling pulling (elbow flexors). **Most compatible:** FL with handstand (opposite shoulder planes). [I] Planche and lever can share a block: Merrick alternates them (S35), GMB rotates planche with pulling (S22).
- **Clustering (coaches):** 2-3 full-body days or a straight-arm/bent-arm split; a true balance skill (handstand) can be daily if not fatiguing (Low, S21); GMB goes daily only after months and says stop when cooked rather than force volume on tired joints (S22); CrossFit: if recovery drops, cut sessions (S34). I found no trial of one straight-arm day versus daily exposures; with volume equal, frequency made no strength difference (S9, PR, 22 studies).
- **Fight sets the week:** sparring first, then strength; protect neck, grip, wrist; strikers overuse the front of the shoulder (Jonathan Pain, CSCS, S36, P). Aerobic + strength: max strength and size not compromised; explosive strength fell, mainly same-session, not at ≥3 h gaps (S10, PR, 43 studies). Running hurt strength more than cycling in S11 (PR, 21 studies) but S10 found no type effect. Low: 4-6 h between strength and endurance (S21). AM/PM slots give that gap. [PR, I]

**Maintenance dose (least that holds a level for 8 weeks)**
| Skill | Best-supported minimum | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| General strength | Young adults kept strength and size up to 32 weeks on 1 session/week, 1 set/exercise if relative load stayed high (S5; "insufficient data for athletes"). S6 (trial, n=70): one-ninth dose kept muscle size, strength largely kept even with no training | PR |
| Handstand | Beginners improved on two 45-min sessions/week; a 2-week skill break did not slow them when pushing strength was trained (S8, n=41 hobbyists, short) | PR |
| FL / planche | 1-3 low-volume exposures/week, warm-up or session start (Low, second-hand, S37); these decay fastest (S37). Keep the current step, shorten sets, not the lever (from S5) | P, L, I |
| Bar MU | 1 weekly set of singles or explosive pulls | I |

Time off costs strength in proportion to its length (S7, PR meta-analysis, 103 studies). I found no retention data for tendon-loaded statics.

## 4. His level (170 cm; 12-15 strict pull-ups; 10 L-sit pull-ups; 50 push-ups; 30 s handstand, poor line; no toes-to-bar; stiff pancake)
- **Closest: front lever.** Pull-ups carry over and there is no balance demand; planche is a "true zero starting point" (Anton, S29, P). Probably tuck/advanced tuck now. [I]
- **Next: bar muscle-up.** Pulls clear GMB's 5 and sit near Glassman's 15. Unknowns: strict dips, chest-to-bar, shoulder extension, false grip. [I]
- **Furthest: planche.** Straight-arm push strength and wrist tolerance start near zero; 50 push-ups and a 30 s handstand do not show lean strength. [I]
- **Shared limiter:** stiff pancake and toes-to-bar block straddle press, straddle shapes and L-sit; fixing them serves three goals. [I]
- At 170 cm leverage is not against him (GMB: taller athletes need more work, S23; S17 simulation). Eight weeks is about one step per skill at best. [P, I]
- **Ask him:** strict bar and ring dips, chest-to-bar pulls, bodyweight, elbow/wrist/shoulder history (prior injury OR 4.1 in S1), fight date and sparring days.

## 5. Honest limits
- **Unknown:** upper-limb tendon time course; whether 10-20 s holds suit tendon (lab protocols use ~3 s repeats); static-skill retention; time-to-first-muscle-up; any data on these skills beside fighting.
- **Conflicts of interest:** Low (books, programmes), GMB (courses, $95 muscle-up course), FitnessFAQs ($97 programmes), Anton (homepage sells coach mentorship, self-reported $430K first-year revenue, and 1:1 coaching), Berg and Calisthenicmovement (apps), Get Gymnast Fit (courses, $69.90/month), Sommer (GymnasticBodies; Ferriss declares no affiliation). razpilosof.com leaks LLM scaffolding ("For the generator database, tag...") so I discarded it.
- **Survivorship:** "zero to full planche in 5 months" (S29) is one student, and Anton's own description says the plan may not transfer. S1 is a social-media survey; injured people respond more.
- **Disagreements:** bands for muscle-up (GMB no; CrossFit, Anton yes); kipping (GMB, Sommer no; Berg early yes); planche frequency (GMB 3, FitnessFAQs 4); muscle-up timing (Sommer 6+ months out; Glassman 15/15; GMB 5/5); planche time (GMB under 2 years; Sommer 4-6 years on rings).
