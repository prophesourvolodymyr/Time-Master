Opinion, not research. All 143 pages of `Mobility & Flexibility` were read through the `tmax` command (title, level, targets, cues, notes). Evidence tags are not used here. ## Day rules
- Wrestler warm-up circuit (dive bombers, walk-outs, kick-throughs) load the wrists and shoulders: use it on wrestling days, not before handstand or planche work.
- PNF pancake (push 5-10 s at up to about half effort, relax, go deeper, 2-3 rounds): the Calixpert page says twice a week is enough and to do it after training. Other evenings: easy holds.
- No long static stretch before sprints, plyos, handstand or boxing. Dynamic moves only.
- Picks follow the Tom Fysh note: rotation first (hips, shoulders), flexion and extension for the spine.

## Opinion on the reference notes
- **Static vs Dynamic Stretching: Nervous System View:** half right. Range is limited a lot by tolerance, so PNF and active end-range work make sense. 'Dynamic beats static' is oversold: dynamic before, static after is the safe split.
- **Russian Wrestler Mobility Routine, GGG Dynamic Stretch Warm-Up, Mobility Routine for Combat Athletes:** the three most usable. The wrestler and the champion are not verified as the true source; treat them as good circuits by creators.
- **PNF Pancake (Calixpert):** the only note with a real dose. Keep.
- **McGregor Movement Routine, Phase SiX, Freedom Movement:** fun movement culture, no dose, wrist-heavy. Reference only.
- **Jefferson Curl:** loaded spine flexion; the 'bulletproof back' claim is not proven. Not for these 8 weeks.
- **Why Toes Matter / PVC pipe foot rolling:** claims, no proof. Skip.
