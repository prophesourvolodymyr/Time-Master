# Boxing evidence for the 8-week camp (agent BoxingBeginner, 2026-11-17)

Tags: **[PR]** peer-reviewed (FT = full text opened, ABS = abstract only) · **[GB]** governing-body/federation document · **[PRAC]** named coach/academic · **[LORE]** repeated, no primary source · **[INF]** my reasoning. My four sub-agents wrote no files, so nothing from them is used. After the rate-limit cut I added six lookups (Chottidao 2022, Smith 2025, ACSM 2021, two agility meta-analyses, Collins 2014 with Elliott 2021, sibling reports).

## 0. Bottom line
1. **No source I opened treats a first bout after 8 weeks as normal.** England Boxing's adult-novice route ("For One Night Only", FONO): minimum 12 weeks, 2–3 sessions/week (24–36 sessions), Level-2 coach, regular technical/conditioned spars to find a safe pairing; a boxer may be refused because "there is not a safe pairing" [GB, FT]. The earlier camp had ≈24 boxing sessions (Mon PM, Wed PM, Thu PM light × 8 weeks; DESIGN.md): FONO's minimum count in 8 calendar weeks [INF]. Readiness is a coach test, not a date.
2. **The priority order is the same in five systems** (Canada, USA, England, Russian federal programme, Cuban school): stance/footwork → straight punches → defences → partner drills and pads → technical sparring → open sparring.
3. **Boxing science is thin.** Trials in boxers: n = 8–24, mostly experienced or elite adults, 1–8 weeks, often uncontrolled. His distrust is partly right. Sturdy facts: the bout's physiology (≥90 % VO2max in a 3×3 simulation) and that leg/hip power tracks punch force.
4. **"One best exercise per purpose" cannot be tested for most boxing drills**: I found one head-to-head (plyometrics vs rope-less rope-jumping, 24 junior boxers: no difference) and no trial ranking a technique drill, ladder or reflex tool. The few trials that exist (all-out punching intervals, lower-limb power) use multi-purpose work, which fits his "two birds" idea.
5. **Calisthenics strength ≠ punch power.** Upper-body maximal strength was unrelated to punch impact; lower-body strength/power was related (Beattie & Ruddock 2022).

## 1. Priority stack, camp shape, tiers (Q1)
**Order taught** [GB unless marked]
- Boxing Canada NCCP 2024 (FT): stance → footwork → jab → jab defences → jab to body → rear straight and its defences → double jab/1-2 → counters → covering up → pads; no hooks/uppercuts yet; "no athlete should ever be faced with an attack for which he/she has not learnt an adequate defense".
- USA Boxing manual 2014: drills → technical spar → open spar. Its Cuba/Russia pages are style blurbs without training numbers; the manual also promotes a sponsor's custom mouthguard (conflict of interest).
- Russian federal initial-stage programme (Korzhenevsky & Kurguzov, FCPSR 2021; **children/youth**, 4.5 sessions/week, not adult data): stance/steps → straight punches to head with defences → to body → combinations; agreed bout («условный бой») first in week 11, free sparring («вольный бой») week 21, first classification bout ≈ month 8; year-1 time split technique 36–42 %, general physical 32–35 %, special physical 10–14 %, tactics 1–4 %; competition "не ранее, чем через три месяца" (no earlier than 3 months after starting).
- Cuban school (Fontanills Quesada 2002, one academic coach [PRAC]): stance → steps (flat, pendulum, turns) → left straight + parries → right straight → hooks → swings → combinations; each element repeated through «escuela de boxeo» (gloveless solo/partner) → «escuela de combate» (gloved pairs, imposed tasks) → free glove work → apparatus; "ejercicios que provoquen la fatiga antes de comenzar la enseñanza, no favorece al proceso" (fatigue before teaching does not help).

**Camp shapes** (regulatory or opinion; none compared experimentally)
| Source | Shape |
|---|---|
| FONO [GB, FT] | 12 wk; 2–3 sessions/wk; "mainly straight punches with appropriate defences and counters"; technical/conditioned spars decide pairings; strength, power, weight loss monitored |
| Witherspoon Jr. 12-week outline [PRAC, gym marketing] | wk 1–4 base (volume, 2–3 best punches, controlled technical spars); 5–8 harder sparring, intervals "matched to round length"; 9–11 peak, volume down; 12 taper, sparring stops by mid-week |
| Ruddock 2016, pro camps 8–12 wk [PR, FT, narrative] | sprint intervals first, HIIT ≈ 6 wk, taper last 2 wk at −40–60 % load |

**How long coaches say** [PRAC, gyms that sell classes]: Ironhand (Sampaga): first light sparring after 3–6 months at 2–4 sessions/week; amateur debut ≥1 year plus months of sparring, "many need two years"; Witherspoon Jr.: 2–3 years. Earlier camp note (Patterson, not reopened): 18–30 months for an athletic adult with no martial art; 6–12 for a former college wrestler.

**Mistakes/time-wasters:** rear-hand power sought early ("one of the biggest handicaps", Boxing Canada); hooks, uppercuts, Ali shuffle early (Fran; BC: pendulum steps "sparingly"); sparring before defences (BC); sparring volume ("quality … more important than quantity", USA); pads with an unskilled holder (BC: "counterproductive"); extra hard work in the final week (Witherspoon; Ruddock taper); acute weight cut (§8); neglected hands (§2).

**Tiers: what the evidence can support**
| Tier | Evidence-supported advice | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| (a) drills only, no technique | The full sequence above. 8 weeks < FONO 12 wk, < Russian free-spar wk 21, < coaches' "3–6 months to first light spar" | no study of adult novices' time-to-ready |
| (b) basic punches + footwork, some bag, no partner work | Skip stage 1; priority = defences, partner/pad rounds, then technical sparring once the coach's checklist is passed [INF: 2–4 weeks] | no numeric dose in any source |
| (c) has sparred | Only expert opinion (USA sample week, ARP, coach checklist); physiology (§3) applies fully | weakest tier |

Sequence is evidence-backed for (a)/(b); dosing for none; (c) is opinion.

## 2. Sparring: dose, hardness, safety, substitutes (Q2)
**Dose.** No source gives a validated rounds/week for a novice [verified absence]. USA Boxing sample club week: Mon drills; Tue technical spar; Wed open spar (100 % skill, 50–75 % speed/power); Thu technical or drills; Fri open spar. Sparring "a few more rounds than the bout", rounds equal or slightly longer, rest cut to 30 s for conditioning [GB]. Boxing Canada: technical sparring only at first, partner's gloved palm is the head target until all fundamental defences are mastered. England Boxing: Level-2 coach, medical; a KO or stopped spar triggers suspension and concussion protocol (rule 5.7). Ironhand's clearance checklist: guard up in round 4, reflexive defence, can throw at ~40 % on command, three rounds of fitness, takes instruction, ego in check, comfortable being hit [PRAC].

**Head exposure.** Jansen 2021 [PR, FT; 23 amateurs, 53 spars + 6 bouts, mouthguard sensors, video-verified]: men's boxing sparring 18.4 ± 13.1 impacts/athlete/session (5 athletes, 10 sessions), median 19.7 g and 1534 rad/s²; bouts hit harder than spars (+2.9 g, CI 0.6–5.4; +456 rad/s², CI 217–737). Stojsih 2010 [PR, ABS; 60 amateurs]: peaks 191 g / 17,156 rad/s² (men), most impacts below the mild-injury threshold, delayed-memory score fell after sparring. [INF] 2 spars/week × 8 weeks ≈ 290 impacts.

**Long-term risk, both directions.** *Against volume:* Stiller 2014 [PR, FT partial; 237 professionals, cross-sectional, self-report]: higher Cumulative Sparring Index (sessions/wk × rounds × intensity 1–4 × years) → worse cognition and balance, decline above CSI ≈150. Neselius 2012 [PR, FT partial; 30 Olympic boxers with ≥45 bouts vs 25 controls]: CSF NFL 532 ± 553 vs 135 ± 51 ng/L, GFAP 496 ± 238 vs 247 ± 147; NFL/GFAP still raised after ≥14 days' rest. *For:* Loosemore 2007 [PR, FT partial]: 36 studies of poor quality (median 2/6); only 4 of 17 better-quality studies showed any chronic injury sign: "no strong evidence". Loosemore 2015 [PR, FT]: GB squad, 5 years, concussion 0.53/1000 h, none in training. [INF] An 8-week camp's CSI (2 spars × 6 rounds × intensity 3 × 0.154 y) ≈ 5.5 vs sample median 90, minimum 4: the cumulative data concern years, not camps. The dominant injury is the hand: 69 hand + 31 wrist of 297 injuries; wrist 60 days lost each.

**Bodies' rules** (ARP consensus 2019: "majority … an amalgamation of non-combat sports recommendations"): any concussion sign → stop; TKO → no sparring 30 days, KO without loss of consciousness 60, with it 90. ARP position: oversized sparring gloves, "minimize head shots during training".

**Substitutes.** *Can:* partner drills; pads (BC: "fairly rapid improvements", timing, decisions, reaction cues; "counterproductive" with an inexperienced holder); conditioned sparring; shadow-sparring facing a partner just out of range (England Boxing resource). *Cannot:* a real opponent's pace and pressure. Contests > sparring > simulations on lactate/CK/cortisol (Finlay 2023, lactate SMD 4.62 [PR, ABS]); gym sparring lactate 9.4 vs lab 6.1 mmol/L (Arseneau 2011); novice activity:rest decays 16:1 → 8:1 → 6:1 over rounds (Slimani 2017). Reflex tools, video: no boxing trial in my sources [not verified].

## 3. Demands of the bout and conditioning (Q3)
Format: novice classes often 3×2 min, elite 3×3, 1-min rests (Chaabene 2015, AIBA rules of its date): confirm his.
| Source | Sample / format | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Davis 2014 [PR, ABS] | 10 novices (VO2max 59.8 ± 4.3), 3×2 min semicontact vs pads | Waer 526.0 ± 57.1, WPCr 58.1 ± 13.6, W[lactate] 26.2 ± 7.1 kJ; ~70 kJ of the aerobic term is PCr resynthesis in the breaks; Wtot ≈680 kJ = **77 % aerobic / 19 % PCr / 4 % lactate** |
| Chaabene 2015 [PR, FT] | review | "mean 85 %" aerobic ([INF] ≈526/610 kJ without the break term); work:rest 9:2 (3×3), 8:3 (4×2), 6:2 (3×2); "decisive actions … anaerobic"; wrestling "slightly more anaerobic" (Wingate) |
| El-Ashker 2018 [PR, FT] | 11 elite, 3×3 vs pads | VO2 55.3/53.8/50.4 ml/kg/min = 101/99/92 % of treadmill VO2max (55.5); HR peak 90/91/92 % HRmax; ≈51–54 punches (about half lead-hand), ≈28 combinations (12–13 two-punch, 4–5 with ≥3), 9–12 defensive actions per round |
| Arseneau 2011 [PR, ABS] | 9 regional boxers | sparring 69.7 ± 8.0 %, pads 66.1 ± 8.0 % VO2peak; bag 39.8/48.8/61.7 % at 60/120/180 punches/min |
| Slimani 2017 [PR, ABS] | review | activity:rest 18:1 elite vs 9:1 novice; triple-punch and counter combinations linked with winning |

**Reconciling 77 % aerobic with a 78 % glycolytic wrestling figure** (Shiyan 2019 not opened; ISSN 2025 seen only through the ResearchAudit report, local://camp-research-audit.md; reasoning [INF]):
(a) The boxing papers agree: high aerobic load (66–70 % VO2peak, >90 % VO2max, 77–86 % of energy). The 14.5/78.2/7.3 split is another sport; no boxing paper reports it. ISSN point 2 is hedged ("can rise to" >70 % beyond 4 min), names no sport or method, and describes Davis's rounds as 3 min although Davis is 3×2 (per the audit).
(b) Shares depend on what is counted: Davis books aerobic PCr resynthesis in the breaks as "aerobic" (≈70 of 680 kJ).
(c) Boxing numbers probably understate glycolysis: all were measured against pads, and real contests/sparring give bigger lactate responses (Finlay; Arseneau 9.4 vs 6.1 mmol/L). Davis's novices (VO2max ≈60) are fitter than most beginners.
(d) Energy share does not rank training methods. Chaabene: decisive actions are anaerobic; Davis: aerobic capacity is the prerequisite for a high work rate and for PCr recovery. Three-minute rounds near bout pace plus short all-out bursts train both.
Net: the ranking barely changes. It argues against calling low-rate bag work conditioning and against steady running as the main method; not against running.

**Conditioning, ranked by fit to the demand**
| # | Method | Evidence | Conf |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Round-format high-intensity work (moving pads, sparring, high-output bag) | Kamandulis 2018 [PR, ABS]: 18 experienced boxers, 4 wk, 3 rounds × 14 × (3 s all-out punching, 10 s rest), 3×/wk (≈2 min all-out/session): arm-crank VO2peak, peak power, punch force and its maintenance ↑, control group unchanged. El-Ashker & Nasr 2012 (via Chaabene): 8 wk/32 sessions of boxing, VO2max 58.2 → 64.6 | low–mod |
| 2 | Sprint/interval running | Usher & Babraj 2025 [PR, FT partial]: 9–10 pros, 3 wk, 3 × 30 s all-out bike ×3/wk, time-to-exhaustion 662 → 702 s (no change over 3-wk control; fixed order). Franchini 2019 [PR, ABS]: 9 HIIT studies, 228 athletes (18 boxers), VO2max/peak +4.4–23.0 %, most consistent gain anaerobic power/capacity | low–mod |
| 3 | Easy aerobic running | Physiology (Davis; Guidetti ranking correlation via Chaabene) and tradition; Soviet "aerobic base in general stage, intervals in special stage" (Kurguzov & Rusanov 1988, secondary); no trial vs alternatives; GB squad ran 4–6 km almost daily, lower-limb injuries 21.6 % | low |
| 4 | Circuits, burpees, "Soviet 4 drills × 30 s" | no boxer trial found [INF] | very low |
Real rounds hold ≈17 punches/min, while bag work at 120–180/min is far above bout punch output: conditioning stress in a round comes from moving, defending and pressure, not punch count [INF].

## 4. Punch power and speed (Q4)
**Determinants.** Soviet Filimonov et al. (NSCA J 1985): legs 38.46 %, trunk rotation 37.42 %, arm extension 24.12 % of punch power [secondary via Bourne 2002; primary not opened]. Dunn et al. 2022 (via Beattie & Ruddock) [PR]: elite amateur men, lower-body IMTP absolute peak force r = 0.68 (46 % of variance); relative force not significant; the high-impact group was heavier (78.4 ± 11.0 vs 61.8 ± 9.4 kg): body mass confounds. Loturco 2016 (n = 15 national team, via B&R): CMJ height r = 0.72 (jab) / 0.80 (cross); loaded jump squat 0.83–0.85; isometric bench press unrelated. Pierce 2006 (via Chaabene): in real bouts nearly all punches were far below laboratory maxima; the winner landed more cumulative force and more punches. Technique: arm alone gives no power (BC). No study compares technique with strength in novices [verified absence]; all elite samples.

**Methods, ranked by evidence** (all small; most uncontrolled)
| # | Method | Best evidence | Home equivalent [INF] |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lower-limb power at optimum load | Loturco 2021 [PR, ABS]: 8 elite, 3 sessions in 1 week, punch impact ≈ +8 %, jump squat +12 %, half squat +14 %, transference ≈0.80; no control stated | jump squats, bounds, hill sprints |
| 2 | All-out punching bursts | Kamandulis 2018 (controlled, n = 18) | band/bag bursts |
| 3 | Multi-component strength + plyo + core + speed | Zhang 2026 [PR, FT partial]: 18 national-team, 3 wk, no control; squat ES 1.72; dominant hook 32.8 → 50.6 kW (ES 1.98); 3-min cumulative punch power ES 1.12; proprietary glove accelerometer; abstract and table disagree on per-punch labels | mix of the above |
| 4 | Plyometrics with external weights | Bruzas 2018 [PR, ABS]: 8 experienced boxers, 12 sessions/4 wk: single-punch force and frequency unchanged; punching power endurance ↑. Disconfirms "plyos raise peak punch force" | clap push-ups, jumps |
| 5 | Rotational med-ball throws, bands/overload, heavy bag | no boxer trial found; advocated from theory (dynamic correspondence, "stiffening" at impact; Ruddock 2016, B&R) | bands, rings |
| 6 | Weights for punch speed (Solovey 1983) | secondary only (Bourne) | – |
| 7 | Overcoming isometrics | none in boxers; B&R say terminal-position isometrics "may be added", from a double-peak EMG observation in MMA fighters | – |
Time to effect: 1–4 weeks in elite athletes (uncontrolled); novices unknown. Filimonov's figure is the only Russian punch-biomechanics number I found.

## 5. Popular drills: transfer (Q5)
| Drill in the camp | Evidence | Verdict | Conf |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stance, step-drag, pivots, 4-direction steps | in every syllabus, no trial; learned inside partner/pad work (Cuban continuity principle) | keep, fold into rounds | mod (consensus) |
| Defence drills with partner | BC: defences before sparring; Russian partner drills in week 2 | top priority | mod |
| Combination ladder to 1-2-5-2 | simulated rounds had 12–13 two-punch and 4–5 longer combinations (El-Ashker); triple/counter combinations linked with winning (Slimani) | keep 1, 1-2, 1-1-2 + counters; long ladders overshoot [INF] | low–mod |
| Slip rope / band line | no study; no punching opponent | plausible, low | low |
| Jump rope | Chottidao 2022 [PR, FT partial]: 24 junior amateurs (12–18 y, 3–5 y boxing), randomised, 8 wk × 3 × 30 min, same 72–106 foot contacts; the "rope" group jumped **without a rope**. Both groups: jab velocity 4.2→4.6 vs 4.3→4.5 m/s (time effect p = 0.039), reaction time 0.24→0.20 vs 0.25→0.22 s (p = 0.047); punch force unchanged; no group × time interaction and no non-training control (gains may be ordinary boxing training or test learning) | warm-up/filler; transfer unproven | low |
| Ladder, coordination steps | Li 2026 [PR, FT partial; 16 RCTs, team-sport athletes, no boxers]: pre-planned change-of-direction g = 0.84, reactive agility g = 0.41 (CI −0.31 to 1.12, n.s.); certainty low (agility), very low (sprint, power); every prediction interval includes 0. Carvajal-Espinoza 2025 [PR, ABS]: change-of-direction speed vs agility r = 0.46 (21 studies, 945 athletes): distinct qualities. Zhang's hexagon-jump gain (ES 1.73) is on the trained test | pre-planned drills improve pre-planned tests; boxing defence is reactive, so likely wasted vs pad work [INF] | low |
| Reflex ball, double-end bag, weighted shadow, "Soviet" 4 × 30 s, overcoming isometrics | none found (Hallmark's "shadow vest" is anecdote, Bourne) | unproven | very low |

Motor learning: Brady 2004 meta-analysis [PR, FT]: 139 effect sizes; lab 0.57 vs applied 0.19; adults in field 0.35; random practice "counterproductive during the early stages", novices should start blocked (Hebert 1996 as quoted). A session of 20+ rotating drills is maximal interleaving, with a small applied benefit and a poor fit for a novice [INF].
Platonov vs "train fresh": a Russian page quoting Keller & Platonov 1993 puts training "в состоянии компенсированного и некомпенсированного утомления" (under fatigue) at stage 4, after the skill is stabilised; Cuban text says fatigue before teaching hurts [LORE/secondary; PRAC]. Both can be right at different stages.

**Minimal session skeleton, ≈75 min** [INF, not a programme]: 8 min warm-up (rope allowed); 15 min technique block, **two** themes, blocked, slow → fast, mirror; 20 min partner/pad rounds (3 on/1 off) on the same two themes plus defence; 12–15 min conditioned/technical sparring if cleared, else more pads; 6–8 min Kamandulis finisher (14 × 3 s all-out, 10 s rest, 3 rounds) or one 3-min round at bout pace; hands/wrists. Ladders, slip rope, reflex tools only if a coach names a fault.

## 6. Neck (Q6)
Eckner 2014 [PR, FT partial]: 46 contact-sport athletes aged 8–30 (5 martial artists, 2 wrestlers; no boxers), lab 1-kg weight drop through a headgear cable, loads "well below" injury. Stronger necks → smaller head velocity change (r = 0.417–0.657; lateral-flexion models not significant, p = 0.09/0.08); pre-tensing cut linear/angular velocity change 12.3 %/9.7 %. Cross-sectional, no training, no concussion outcome. *For, with outcome data:* Collins 2014 [PR, ABS]: 6,704 high-school athletes (soccer, basketball, lacrosse): each extra pound of neck strength → concussion odds −5 % (OR 0.95, CI 0.92–0.98). Elliott 2021 [PR, ABS]: of 6 studies, 4 interventions that included neck exercises cut head/neck injuries and concussion, but inside broader programmes. *Against:* Smith 2025 [PR, FT partial]: 28 law-enforcement cadets, 3–5 sparring rounds of 2–3 min per day, instrumented mouthguards: neck strength unrelated to the count or size of head impacts; larger neck girth went with more impacts. All observational or multi-component; no boxers. Verdict: plausible, cheap, unproven for boxing; anticipatory bracing is a skill; low value per minute [INF].

## 7. Boxing-only vs MMA, wrestling overlap (Q7)
Boxing-only: drop wrestling, clinch and square stance; the wrestling hours go to partner rounds [INF]. MMA: Del Vecchio 2011 [PR, FT]: 26 regional matches, 52 pros of black-belt level, 3×5 min; high-intensity standing efforts 6–9 s, groundwork 8–14 s; half ended in high-intensity groundwork; 57.7 % KO; not novice amateurs. England Boxing converts experience: 1–10 wrestling bouts = 1 boxing contest, MMA contest = 0.75 [GB, FT]: wrestling is weak evidence of boxing skill. Fran (boxing coach, "no significant expertise" in MMA): boxing stance at mid/short range is "an absolute liability" in MMA [PRAC opinion]. Jansen: MMA bouts 3773 vs boxing bouts 1642 rad/s² (2 vs 4 sessions). Joint progress in 8 weeks: no data; practice hours are the limit, and each art gets about half of them [INF].

## 8. Weight cut (Q8)
Hall & Lane 2001 [PR, ABS]: 16 experienced amateurs lost 5.16 % body mass in a week by food/fluid restriction: worse 4×2-min circuit performance, more anger/fatigue/tension. Chaabene 2015 [FT]: Brazilian Olympic boxers all cut (mean 5.8 kg); Reljic 2013: a 5.6 ± 1.7 % 5-day cut was mostly water (plasma volume −8.6 ± 3.9 %), normal a week later; Smith 2001: 2.95 ± 1.08 % did not change punching; authors read <4 % as tolerable, ≥5 % untested. ACSM consensus 2021 [PR, FT partial; expert statement]: acute fluid/sweat loss of 2–3 % body mass, as in routine training, has "few disadvantages" with aggressive recovery; >3 % "may have substantial negative implications to health and performance"; the gradual route it cites is ≤1.5 % per week (Wisconsin wrestling programme); safer rules move the weigh-in close to the event. ARP (position, not a trial): ≤3 % at weigh-in. Amateur weigh-ins are the morning of the bout (Witherspoon [PRAC]), so no rehydration window [INF]. Practice: pick a class near natural weight, no dehydration cut.
