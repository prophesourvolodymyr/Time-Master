# Exercise selection, time, concurrency, skill and load: evidence for the athlete discussion

Tags: **[PR]** peer-reviewed (journal, year, design, n) · **[PRAC]** named practitioner or secondary Russian source · **[LORE]** repeated, no primary source · **[INF]** my reasoning · **[REL]** relayed by Main, not opened by me · **[SNIP]** seen only in a search snippet. Russian passages are my own translations. Athlete: fit, non-elite, 4 goals, 6 days × (1 h + 1.5 h), 8 weeks.

## 0. Read first
- Nearly all trials below are in untrained or recreationally active adults, or gym lifters. None I opened tests beginner fighters training twice a day. Every application to him is [INF].
- Correction to the brief: Nielsen 2014 is an exploratory cohort (n = 874 novice runners), not an RCT. The RCT on the 10% rule is Buist 2008 (n = 532).
- I found no study on (a) exercises per session, (b) goals per 8-week block, (c) the productive share of a mixed session. Those answers are inference from adjacent evidence.
- Platonov, the author his plan follows, restricts deep-fatigue loading to advanced stages (§5).

## 1. Two birds with one stone
The belief hides two claims. At **exercise level** it is partly true. At **session level** the evidence and a Russian wrestling source point the other way.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Kettlebell swing vs jump squat, 21 men, 6 wk, 2×/wk [PR Lake & Lauder 2012, RCT, no no-training control] | Half-squat 1RM +9.8%, jump height +19.8%; no difference between methods. One ballistic hinge served strength and power. |
| 10×6 s kicks / 10 s rest vs 10×35 m sprints / 10 s rest, 24 taekwondo athletes aged ~16, 4 wk [PR Ouergui 2023, RCT] | Same lactate and peak-heart-rate adjustment. Only the technique group lowered RPE and raised combined attacks; the sprint group's single attacks fell. A real two-for-one: sprints trained no technique. |
| Rotational vs linear S&C vs control, 31 boxers with ≥1 yr experience, 8 wk [PR Pinto 2026] | Punch power rose in every group, controls too; rotational best, mainly hooks (group×time p < 0.05). |
| Uphill–downhill sprinting vs flat, 22 studies [PR Sports Med 2023 meta-analysis] | SMD −0.41 (−0.79 to −0.03) for uphill–downhill; resisted methods including uphill −0.14 (−0.36 to 0.07), trivial. Sprint speed only; fight-conditioning value untested. |
| Loaded carries, sandbag work, sprawl conditioning, dummy throws | Strongman review: 11 articles, biomechanics only [PR Hindle 2019]. No training-effect trial found. [INF] only. |
| Complexes (heavy + light in one workout), 33 studies, n = 1,064 [PR Bauer 2019] | Beat traditional training only for squat 1RM and 20 m sprint, driven by single studies. Saves time; adds little potency. |
| Circuits, 8 + 8 studies, healthy adults [PR Muñoz-Martínez 2017] | VO₂max and bench 1RM rose vs controls; never compared with dedicated training. |

**Session level.** Concurrent training leaves maximal strength (SMD −0.06, 95% CI −0.20 to 0.09) and size (−0.01) intact on average but blunts explosive strength (−0.28, −0.48 to −0.08). The loss is −0.31 (−0.62 to −0.01) when sessions are ≤20 min apart and not significant at ≥3 h [PR Schumann 2022, 43 studies, n = 1,090, mean PEDro 4.3/10]. In trained people, lower-body 1RM fell only for same-session work (−0.66 vs −0.10) [PR Petré 2021, 27 studies]. Russian wrestling practice: "each session solves one priority task, all other tasks are background"; alactic before glycolytic, a little aerobic before alactic, a rest day after three training days [REL Shiyan 2019]. Verkhoshansky, in Cherkashin's summary, says high-volume mixed loading sets systems against each other [PRAC]. Platonov rejects strictly sequential development, yet writes that "against a background of parallel development of all sides… certain qualities are developed preferentially, with concentration of the corresponding means" [PRAC]. Every Russian source is therefore priority-plus-background.

**When overlap works.** When both goals share the force–velocity–pattern (the five criteria, §2). It fails when stimuli differ: rep ranges far apart (≤5 vs ≥10) give different adaptations [PR Stone 2022, narrative review]. The asymmetry matters: strength-only training gave power ES 0.91, endurance-only 0.11 [PR Wilson 2012, 21 studies]. [INF] At 12–15 pull-ups and 50 push-ups, bodyweight sets of those lifts sit at the endurance end of the continuum, so "heavy calisthenics" at bodyweight is a strength-endurance stimulus, not a maximal-strength one.

**Lost by combining:** explosive-strength gains (contested: the 2026 umbrella review in §12 found power comparable); skill acquisition when local muscle fatigue comes first (§5); dose control per quality; supersets raise RPE. **Gained:** time.

## 2. "A single best exercise"
**Supported: specificity.** Transfer = gain in performance ÷ gain in the trained exercise (Zatsiorsky, quoted by Young). Eight weeks of squats gave +21% squat 1RM, +21% vertical jump, +2.3% 40 m sprint; bilateral squats and jump squats transferred minimally to sprinting, unilateral and horizontal plyometrics did (+2.6% at 10 m); a bench-press throw at 60% 1RM moved the bar at 0.4 m/s against 11.4 m/s in a netball pass [PR Young 2006, narrative review]. Weights lifted rose ~200% while isometric force rose 15–20%; the rest was task-specific coordination [PR Rutherford & Jones 1986, n = 32].

**Not supported: a *single* best.** I found no trial ranking exercises by Verkhoshansky's criteria: (1) amplitude and direction, (2) accentuated part of the movement, (3) magnitude and time of force, (4) speed to maximal force, (5) regime of muscle work [PRAC Mehrikadze 2001; PR Stone 2022]. Stone notes criterion 5 is questioned; Goodwin & Cleather add a sixth. Other Russian authors list other criteria (Gridasova & Umarov; Popov: exceed competitive intensity by up to 10%) [PRAC], so there is no single Russian standard. Mehrikadze, citing Kozlov & Muravyev: sled towing helps support-phase muscles but harms the swing leg, so one exercise can help one phase and disturb another [PRAC, no data].

**Where choice matters** (sizes from the studies; the ranking is [INF]):

| # | Choice | Size | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Practise the target movement vs a lookalike | large | Rutherford & Jones; Young (+21% → +2.3%) |
| 2 | Pattern and speed match | large for fast actions | Young 2006 |
| 3 | Load / rep range (≤5 vs ≥10) | large if far apart | Stone 2022; Wilson 2012 |
| 4 | Weightlifting vs traditional lifts for jumping | moderate, imprecise | Morris 2022, 16 studies: CMJ g 0.95 (0.04–1.87); sprint g 1.04 (−0.03 to 2.39) |
| 5 | Rotational vs linear for hooks | moderate | Pinto 2026 |
| 6 | Full vs partial range; push-up vs bench | small to moderate, disputed | Pallarés 2021, 16 studies: full range strength ES 0.56, lower-limb size 0.88; a 2023 meta-analysis reports 0.05–0.2 [SNIP]; push-up ≈ bench at matched 40% 1RM load, n = 18, biceps size rose only with bench [Kikuchi 2017] |
| 7 | Rotating exercises | small; harmful if excessive | Kassiano 2022, 8 studies, N = 241: systematic variation fine; redundant or frequently rotated exercises may hinder |

I found no squat-variant comparison. Training level changes the answer: beginners get good transfer from general work, experienced athletes only from specific work [Young]; squat-strength gain and sprint gain correlated r = −0.77 across 15 studies, moderated by training level (p = 0.03) [PR Seitz 2014]; heavy lifting beat power training in weaker athletes [Stone]. [INF] For him: early on most sensible exercises work; "best" matters more near his ceiling; for the handstand goals the best exercise is the skill itself and its graded regressions.

## 3. Time efficiency
- **Rest.** 3 min beat 1 min for squat and bench 1RM and thigh size in 21 trained men over 8 wk [PR Schoenfeld 2016]. For size, >60 s helps slightly and nothing differs beyond ~90 s [PR Singer 2024, 9 studies]. Both concern gym lifts. [INF] For maximal power and skill, long rest is part of the stimulus.
- **Pairing.** Supersets keep volume and "roughly halve" session time [PR Iversen 2021, narrative; Zhang 2025]; cost: higher perceived exertion and muscle damage.
- **Contrast / PAP.** Acute effect small: jump 0.29, throw 0.26, upper-body ballistic 0.23, sprint 0.51; larger in stronger lifters; longer recovery better (0.44–0.49 vs 0.17); maximal isometric conditioning −0.09 [PR Seitz & Haff 2016, 47 studies, n = 1,954]. A bonus for strong people, not a plan.
- **Cluster sets.** No difference from traditional sets for strength, power, size, 29 studies [PR Davies 2021]. Little relevance to bodyweight skills.
- **Warm-up.** Performance improved in 79% of criteria, 32 studies [PR Fradkin 2010]; keep it exercise-specific [Iversen].
- **Session length / splitting.** Two lower-body sessions 3.5–5 h apart gave 3% more reps (169 vs 164, ES 0.42); 22 of 23 women still preferred one long session [PR Pedersen, crossover, n = 23].
- **Productive fraction.** No study quantifies it. A 58% work / 42% rest split is not by itself waste: planned rest between maximal efforts is stimulus. Waste is unplanned transitions, repeated warm-ups, low-value exercises [INF]. Real MMA competitors (n = 14): 3.9–5.3 h/week, sparring 2–7% and striking drills 20–32% of time [PR Kirk 2021]. His plan is ~15 h/week.

## 4. Concurrent training, goals, two sessions a day
- **Interference.** Strength 5 d/wk + endurance 6 d/wk for 10 wk: VO₂max rose ~25% (bike) with or without strength; strength gains matched strength-only for 7 weeks, then levelled off and declined in weeks 9–10 [PR Hickson 1980]. Running + lifting cut size and strength, cycling did not; interference grew with endurance frequency and duration [PR Wilson 2012]. Type, frequency (>5 vs <5/wk), status and age were not significant moderators [Schumann]. So conditioning may threaten explosive qualities most (the 2026 umbrella review in §12 found power comparable); static strength and size look robust.
- **Block vs parallel.** Multi-targeted blocks beat traditional plans in 28 studies (endurance, team, dual sports, strength/power, recreational); concentrated single-quality blocks in combat and team sports raised fitness tests but not sport-specific performance [PR Issurin 2016, review by the model's author]. Verkhoshansky's 3–4-week blocks with deliberate under-recovery and a 7–10 day pause are for qualified athletes; traditional loading is "justified for medium-qualified athletes and those needing much technique time" [PRAC Cherkashin]. No block-vs-parallel trial in novices. Periodised beat non-periodised for 1RM (ES 0.31, 0.04–0.57); undulating beat linear only in trained (0.61) not untrained (0.06) [PR Moesgaard 2022, 35 studies].
- **Two sessions a day.** In trained men, 2 sessions/day gave squat 1RM +16.1% vs +7.8%, other outcomes equal, 8 wk, n = 23 [PR Corrêa 2022]; ≥3 h apart avoids same-session attenuation (Schumann). Platonov lists sessions per day rising from one to two–three, and per week from two–three to 10–15, as *multi-year* load growth; a jump to 1.5–2× volume helps only if prior preparation supports it, earlier it is "forced preparation" [PRAC]. In 33 triathletes a 3-week overload left 11 of 23 functionally overreached, with a smaller taper gain than non-overreached peers (quoted 2.6 ± 1.1%) and more infections (prevalence 70% vs 20% and 10%) [PR Aubry 2014]. Overtraining means prolonged maladaptation; no marker is reliable [PR Meeusen 2013]. Nobody has tested six days × two sessions in beginners.

## 5. Skill acquisition (novice)
- **Distribution.** Spaced beat massed overall (d = 0.46; 63 studies), but 0.97 for simple motor tasks and 0.11 / 0.07 for complex, physical tasks; low-rigour studies inflated it (1.22 vs 0.40); only 3 of 16 retention effects had >24 h gaps [PR Donovan & Radosevich 1999]. "Frequency beats volume for balance skills" is [LORE]; this meta-analysis does not test it.
- **Blocked vs random.** Overall ES 0.38 (61 studies), applied research 0.19, adults 0.50 [PR Brady 2004]. In sport and PE (36 studies) only 20% of 183 outcomes fit the interleaving advantage; no pre–post difference (p = 0.35); retention favoured random (ES −0.35) but with no overall long-term gain (−0.13, p = 0.18) [PR Ammar 2024; a 2025 reply exists, not opened]. High load helps simple-skill learners; novices in complex skills do better when load is *reduced* [PR Wulf & Shea 2002, review]. [INF] Few techniques, blocked first; interleave once form is stable.
- **Deliberate practice.** Explained 18% of variance in sport (21% music, 26% games) [PR Macnamara 2014; erratum exists, not opened]. It matters; it is not everything.
- **Fatigue.** Pinch-force skill, fatigue to ~60% MVC loss: learning stayed impaired on the unfatigued next day (four experiments, N = 38, 20, 45, 18; a cognitive sequence task was unaffected) [PR Branscheidt 2019]. Across 38 articles / 43 experiments, fatigue harmed acquisition in 65.1%, helped in 9.3%, no effect 16.3%, mixed 9.3%; local neuromuscular fatigue is worst, cardiovascular exertion may aid consolidation; retention under renewed fatigue was tested in only 7 [PR Hassar 2026, systematic review]. A 2026 scoping review calls findings inconsistent, ages 16.5–31 only [PR Goushe 2026]. A Russian note says running while fatigued disturbs support/swing-leg timing [PRAC Mehrikadze, no data].
- **Platonov on tired training.** His chapter calls "pain–agony" fatigue training acceptable for athletes preparing for top results, "absolutely unacceptable" for young athletes in the first three development stages, harmful in speed-strength and complex-coordination disciplines, and wrong for flexibility ("many coaches hold the mistaken rule" that pain-based stretching works). Pronounced load waves with deep fatigue start at stage 3; stages 1–2 are "planned complex technical and functional preparation not tied to maximal loads" [PRAC]. [INF] An adult beginner maps to stages 1–2; the framework describes youth, the mapping is mine.
- **Answer.** Transfer-appropriate practice is a fair case for rehearsing *already-learned* skills tired, late in camp. For acquiring new skills the evidence leans against. Whether tired practice improves fighting is untested.

## 6. How fast does a fit novice adapt in 8 weeks?
- **Strength.** Early gain is largely coordination (Rutherford & Jones). Seven untrained volunteers, 35 days, 3×/wk leg extension: MVC +38.9%, EMG +34.8%, cross-section +3.5–5.2% by day 20 and +6.5–7.4% at day 35, fascicle length +2.4% by day 10 [PR Seynnes 2007]. "Neural first, structural later" is a simplification; where neural adaptation sits is unresolved [PR Škarabot 2021, review].
- **Aerobic.** VO₂max +20–25% in 10 weeks of daily endurance work [Hickson]; HIIT in combat athletes +4.4% to +23.0%, 9 studies [PR Franchini 2019]. Baseline decides.
- **Punching.** Power rose in 8 weeks even in control boxers [Pinto].
- [INF] He is not untrained in push and pull (50 push-ups, 12–15 pull-ups): expect smaller gains there, larger where he is new (heavy and ballistic lifting, boxing, wrestling). No study I opened gives time-to-competence for novice boxing, wrestling or a one-arm handstand.

## 7. Progression, taper, deload
- **10% rule.** A 13-week plan built on the 10% rule vs a standard 8-week plan: injuries 20.8% vs 20.3% (p = .90), 532 novice runners [PR Buist 2008, RCT]. Weekly progression <10%, 10–30%, >30%: no significant difference; distance-related injuries after >30% over two weeks HR 1.59 (0.96–2.66), p = 0.07; authors ask for RCTs [PR Nielsen 2014, cohort, 874 novices]. Running distance only.
- **20% rule / ACWR.** "No week >20% above the last" is [PRAC] (Boxing Science, via the earlier work; not opened). I found no peer-reviewed threshold. Rapid increases likely cause many non-contact soft-tissue injuries; ACWR proposed as best predictor [PR Gabbett 2016, opinion review; Gabbett consults for teams]. Counter: "no evidence supporting the use of ACWR… for training recommendations", no study estimated causal effects, ratio statistics inaccurate [PR Impellizzeri 2020]. Verdict: "avoid spikes" is plausible; any specific percentage is unsupported (§12: a 2025 cohort found no week-to-week effect but a single-session spike effect). Sparring impacts are not distance; no threshold exists.
- **Taper.** Best: 2 weeks, volume cut 41–60% exponentially, intensity and frequency unchanged (overall effect 0.59 for 2 weeks, 0.72 for the cut) [PR Bosquet 2007, 27 studies; sport mix not checked]. Cut volume up to 60–90%, frequency ≤ −20%, gain ≈ 3% (0.5–6%), progressive beats step [PR Mujika & Padilla 2003, review]. Team sports: power SMD 0.44, change of direction 0.38, VO₂max 0.76 [PR Vachon 2021, 14 studies]. 60–83% of triathletes peaked in the first two taper weeks [Aubry]. Real MMA fighters only step-tapered in the last week [Kirk]. No combat-sport taper trial found.
- **Deload.** A 34-coach Delphi defines it as reduced stress to mitigate fatigue and promote recovery [PR Bell 2023]. A full week off mid-way through 9 weeks in 39 trained lifters left size, power and endurance unchanged and cost strength [PR Coleman 2024; authors have commercial ties]. Not tested in a fight camp; a novice trial is in §12. [INF] Decide by readiness, not calendar.

## 8. Direct answers to Main
**Exercise vs session level.** Exercise-level two-for-ones are best supported when conditioning is built from the skill itself (Ouergui) or when both goals are force-power (Lake & Lauder). Mixing neural, skill or power work with glycolytic work in one session is not supported (Schumann, Petré, Shiyan [REL]). Dummy throws, carries and sprawl work are plausible two-for-ones by the pattern criteria [INF], untested.

**Exercises per session.** No trial of 20 vs 5. Adjacent evidence: the minimum effective strength list is three lifts (squat or leg press, a pull, a push) [PR Iversen 2021, narrative]; exercises per session did not significantly moderate sprint gains (p = 0.16) [Seitz 2014]; excessive rotation may hinder gains; early gains are exercise-specific; earlier local fatigue harms later skill learning. [INF] About 3–6 blocks per session (one skill, one strength or power, one conditioning) fits the evidence; 17 sections is outside anything tested.

**Cost of fragmenting.** At 60–90 min, 17 sections is 3.5–5.3 min each and, at 58% work, 2–3 min of work per section [INF arithmetic from Main's figures]. That cannot hold a warm-up and enough repetitions for coordination to form, and later sections run tired.

**Goals that interfere.** [INF] Documented: endurance and glycolytic work vs explosive qualities. Likely: handstand, planche and press vs boxing push work (shared wrist and shoulder tissue, fatigue before balance practice); long static stretching vs power or sparring in the same session. Little expected between static-strength skills and aerobic work. Supportable plan: two priorities (the fight, one handstand target), planche and mobility as background, which is the Russian priority-plus-background rule [REL; Platonov].

## 9. Verdict on the athlete's beliefs
| Belief | Verdict | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Some exercises kill two birds | **Partly** | Works for same-family qualities and technique-built conditioning (Lake & Lauder; Ouergui). Not for neural vs metabolic goals (Schumann; Petré). Gains time, not potency (Bauer; Zhang). |
| One best exercise per purpose | **Partly** | Specificity is large (Young; Rutherford & Jones). No trial ranks exercises; many variants near-equal; training level changes the answer. |
| No minute wasted | **Partly** | Rest between maximal efforts is stimulus (Schoenfeld; Seitz & Haff). Real waste: transitions, repeated warm-ups, 17 sections. |
| Training tired helps | **Not supported for skill, strength, power; partly for planned overload followed by a taper** | Branscheidt; Hassar; Dunn (punch force falls after leg and trunk fatigue); Aubry (overload paid off only without overreaching); Platonov's own limits. No support found for tired flexibility work. |
| Four goals at once | **Not supported beyond ~2 priorities** [INF] | Interference data; Issurin; Shiyan [REL]; Platonov. |

## 10. Bias, conflicts, uncertainty
Issurin authored the block model he reviews. Gabbett consults for teams and champions ACWR. Coleman's authors have commercial ties (Tonal, Renaissance Periodization). Stone co-authored the strength-endurance continuum he reviews. Soviet methods come from champions (survivorship) and from a system with documented doping; I opened no source on doping. Platonov's reported results are a record of outcomes, not a test of his rules (library note). Schumann's trials were medium quality. ## 12. Targeted lookups (added after the first draft)
- **Deload in novices.** 19 untrained young men, 8 wk, leg extension and biceps curl twice a week; each limb randomised to continuous training or to a deload (once a week, 2 sets) in weeks 4 and 8. Similar gains in muscle thickness and 10RM; no condition × time effect (p = 0.239–0.955); every CI included zero [PR Pancar 2026, Sci Rep, within-subject RCT]. A reduced-volume deload neither hindered nor added to novice gains (single-joint lifts, size and 10RM only). Coleman's full week off cost strength in trained lifters. [INF] Use deloads for fatigue and tissue management, not as a gain tool.
- **Concurrent training, newest synthesis (disconfirming for interference).** 17 meta-analyses, 144 studies, 1,492 healthy participants: versus lifting alone, combining improved aerobic capacity (SMD 0.77) while strength, power and size were comparable; versus endurance alone, strength was greater (SMD 0.59); no significant sequence effect, a trend for lifting first (strength SMD 1.69; size 0.83, p = 0.36); highly trained and elite data "remain scarce" [PR Held 2026, umbrella review]. This conflicts with Schumann and Wilson on explosive output. For a recreationally trained person interference is not established; none of it tests skills.
- **Spike rule, newest data.** 5,205 runners, 588,071 sessions, 35% injured: a single session exceeding the 30-day longest run by >10% raised injury rates (HRR 1.64 at >10–30%; 1.52 at >30–100%; 2.28 at >100%). Weekly ACWR showed a *negative* dose-response and the week-to-week ratio no relationship [PR Frandsen 2025, BJSM, cohort; self-reported injury; mean age 45.8]. Weekly-percentage rules (10%, 20%) get no support here; the risk unit is the single session. [INF] Fight-camp analogue: the first hard sparring or wrestling session, or any session far longer than the recent maximum.
- **Novice boxing.** Novice female students: all technical skills improved after a 4-week course (p < 0.001); at 2 months they had fallen 17–22% from the 4-week level but punches stayed +19% to +31% above baseline (stance +3% static, −7% dynamic) [PR Martusciello 2026; n not in the abstract]. Gains come fast for true beginners and fade without practice. [INF] He is not a zero, so expect less headroom.
- **Fatigue and punches.** 28 highly trained amateur boxers: nine 1-min rowing bouts cut peak punch force for every punch type and slowed force development in all but the jab, most for crosses and hooks [PR Dunn 2022; no non-exercise control; non-specific fatigue]. [INF] Punching after leg and trunk work rehearses degraded mechanics; the authors advise careful scheduling of S&C and boxing sessions.
