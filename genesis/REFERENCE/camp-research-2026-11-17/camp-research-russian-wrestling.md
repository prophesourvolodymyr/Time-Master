# Russian/Soviet wrestling training: evidence for the camp discussion

Agent RussianWrestling, 2026-11-17. Tags: **[peer-reviewed]** journal/year/design; **[practitioner]** named coach/athlete or an official coaching document; **[lore]** repeated, no primary source; **[inference]** mine. "Opened" = I read the page; numbers as printed; translations mine (Russian original for key lines). Russian coaching texts describe accepted practice and rarely cite trials. Column D/L: **D** = described in a source I opened (practice or test norm, no efficacy trial); **L** = lore; **D\*** = the athlete's own account. Appendix A holds the six lookups made after the rate-limit cut (Shiyan 2019, energy systems, blocks).

## 1. Bottom line

1. **The owner is right that the earlier work had no Russian source.** But Russian literature is not "better science": it is doctrine, one expert survey (n = 50) and small experiments (n = 5-30) run by the methods' own authors [S2, S5, S36]. Where Russian and Western texts agree (anaerobic power/capacity, strength and power matter; flexibility ranks last; static stretch after, not before) the evidence is firmest [S2, S3, S29].
2. **Time budget (federal model programme, 2021):** general prep (ОФП) 9-28 %, special physical prep (СФП) 18-39 %, technical-tactical 24-39 % at every stage; СФП+ТТП is 42-71 % [S1]. Islam Mamedov on Abdulmanap's sessions: "90 percent of training is wrestling" [S10].
3. **One priority per session is documented** at top stages: «занятия избирательного типа с однородным содержанием … ограниченное число задач»; multi-quality sessions are "rare, mainly in general prep" [S1]; same in Shiyan 2019 [S33]. But the same school selects strength exercises by «координационное сходство с соревновательными движениями» [S2] and uses conjugate/complex work. So its "best exercise for a purpose" is the most wrestling-like one, and "two birds" is the norm near competition: partner exercises were 70-80 % of speed-strength volume in the last 21-28 days [S2].
4. **Fatigue is placed on purpose** (session end, last circuit rounds, successive partners, end of week) for special endurance, willpower and technique-under-fatigue. No source I opened says tired is better for max strength, speed or flexibility (§6).
5. **His general strength already exceeds Russian entry norms** (top stage: pull-ups ≥ 8, push-ups ≥ 48 [S1]); his limiter is wrestling-specific work against resistance [inference].
6. **First-bout framing:** early competitions are «контрольные … без специальной к ним подготовки»; «первое соревнование по взрослым – это разведка» [S1].
7. **What does not transfer is the inputs:** partners and coaches («Мы без своих партнеров подготовиться не можем», Tedeev [S18]), 20-32 h/week, years, selection, recovery infrastructure, pharmacology (§5).
8. **Energy systems:** ">70 % aerobic beyond 4 min" (ISSN 2025) comes from karate/judo/boxing accounting, not wrestling; Shiyan's "78 % glycolytic" is a contribution to a special-endurance score, not energy turnover; both can hold (A2).

**Live vs drill vs conditioning**
- Qualified wrestlers: up to 50 % of technical time on individual actions, 2-3 actions per session, each revisited 3-4 times a week; 35-40 % on technical-tactical chains; 10-15 % on bout tactics (Shakhmuradov 1997 via S2). Opponent modelling starts 2 weeks out.
- Session template: warm-up 15-25 min (wrists, neck isometrics, lumbar, rolls); main 60-80 min = technique 20-30 + physical qualities 20-30 (circuits) + bouts 10-30; cool-down 10-15 (static stretch) [S4].
- Beginners: SFP «нельзя начинать до освоения срединных структур приемов борьбы»; early years = technique, bridges, falls, games [S1]. Eganov via S9: keep drill success at 30-40 % for fastest learning (> 40 % stalls; < 30 % does not stick).

**Periodisation and taper**
- Working vocabulary: Matveyev's wave and Platonov's microcycle types (втягивающий, ударный/развивающий, подводящий, соревновательный, восстановительный) [S2]. Preparatory 3-5 months (general, then special), competitive 2-4, transition 3-4 weeks [S4]. Shiyan's "double week" (3 training days, rest, 2 training days, sauna, rest; two sessions a day) [S33, A1].
- Platonov-school text: one-directional concentrated microcycles in combat sports are «просто недопустимо» [S6]. Blocks do exist in Russian wrestling (strength only, A3) [S36].
- Taper: volume down, intensity kept; Dagbaev's подводящий week still has task bouts at HR ≥ 180 [S2]. Early peaking is a "грубая ошибка" [S4]. Abdulmanap: «за 10 дней до боя максимальные физнагрузки прекращаются, включая бег, последние три дня ноги вообще не напрягаем. Занимаемся клинчем, тактикой» (tied to his water-loading weight cut) [S13]. Seluyanov: direct prep 10-30 days; Polish women's judo squad's total volume fell almost 10× by the Games [S5].

## 5. Q4: transfer to a fit non-elite athlete (bands, bars, rings, parallettes, backyard, hill, gym 1×/week, uncertain partner)

**Plausible with his kit** (documented means; feasibility is [inference]): bridge, neck and rolls on a thin mat; accelerations 20-30 m from varied starts and long jumps in the yard; hill running at max pace; box depth jumps with the documented dose; 30 s/15 s station circuits in bodyweight form; ring/bar work in place of no-leg rope climb; band imitation of throws and underhooks (S1 lists «имитация … с резиновыми амортизаторами»); 400 m repeats and 3-5 km cross. One gym session a week fits Seluyanov's ≤ 1 strength session per muscle group per week (Hartmann & Tünnemann 2002, via S5).

**Needs a partner or room:** partner lifts/carries, circular and task bouts, dummy tests (a 35-40 % body-mass bag is [inference]), game days. Partner work is the specific core of the system; Dagbaev raised it to 70-80 % near competition.

**Does not transfer:** volume (20-32 h/week; 9 months a year in camps); years (Karelin started at 13, won Olympic gold at 21); selection (S16, S18); partner pool (S32); recovery system (7-17 % of time, sauna, massage, medical tests [S1]); pharmacology: the 2021 federal programme lists «использования фармакологических средств восстановления и повышения специальной работоспособности» among planning principles [S1]; Medved's caviar story [S27]; altitude camps; weight-cut protocols.

## Appendix A. Lookups after the rate-limit cut

**A1. Shiyan 2019 [S33]** (opened in full; review paper, Int J Wrestling Sci 9:1, 7-18; RGUFK Moscow). No sample sizes or methods are given for the experiments it cites (figures not visible in my copy); most numbers trace to his 1998 dissertation.
- Verified: «Each training session is dedicated to solving one priority task. All other training tasks are background.» Allowed pairings (after Volkov): a little aerobic before alactic; alactic before glycolytic; a little anaerobic before aerobic.
- Verified: "double week": three training days, a rest day (or fasting day with a little aerobic work), two training days, a sauna and a rest day; "as a rule two workouts per day". The reason given is "incomplete recovery" after three days (no data shown). Alactic work belongs "at the very beginning of the microcycle", fresh; glycolytic work is less effective after an aerobic session.
- Verified, with a caveat: the optimum is 600-650 min/week of *work* (rest excluded), from an experiment he cites without methods (15-20-week macrocycles; optimum 100-140 days); he converts it "very roughly" to two 2-h workouts a day, five days a week.
- Verified: «1:20»: active time to rest "can reach 1:20", e.g. 5 min of maximum work in a 100-min session; he says this makes coaches avoid anaerobic work. Table 1: alactic ≤ 6-10 s, 6-7 reps × 5-6 sets, rest 1-2 / 3-5 min; glycolytic 0.3-2 min, 3-4 reps × 2-3 sets, rests 5-3-2 min, 10-15 between sets.
- Verified: examples: 400 m × 3-4 with rests 3-2-1 min, new series after 7-15 min and HR ~130, 3-10 series; dummy: 3-7 series of 3-5 × [15 max throws + a 1-min bout with a fresh partner], continue while HR returns to 130, stop at first sign of overwork.
- Energy split: 14.5 / 78.2 / 7.3 % (alactic/glycolytic/aerobic) vs Fox & Mathews 1981 (90/10/0). His text calls his split the contribution of each function "to the level of special endurance", i.e. a share of a test score, not energy supplied (method not described) [inference from wording].
- School vs independent: one school (Shiyan; Igumenov & Shiyan 1998; Volkov; Sariev). Outside support is only textbooks, one US judo fibre study (Callister 1991) and judo/karate data. His "aerobic capacity does not predict pH" comes from wrestlers with similar VO2max (48 ± 5), a restricted range [inference]. The federal programme independently has "one task per session" [S1]; Seluyanov disagrees on glycolytic loading [S5]. Tag: [practitioner, author's school, no controls shown].

**A2. Which energy claim is right** [S34, S35, S29]
- ISSN 2025 position stand (a nutrition/weight-cut paper, narrative review): "as duration increases, > 4 min, the aerobic share can rise to > 70 %". Evidence behind it: karate (77.8 % aerobic in 4 min 27 s), judo (70 % oxidative, 21 % ATP-PCr, 8 % glycolytic), boxing. For wrestling it says the precise split "remains challenging", citing a World Championships study where glycolytic was largest, and Kraemer 2004 (lactate up to 20 mmol/L in a 5-min college freestyle match).
- **Verdict for wrestling: unproven either way.** No source measured wrestling by energy accounting: Fox & Mathews is a 1981 table; Shiyan's is a score contribution; ISSN's is extrapolated from other sports.
- Both can hold: energy accounting credits the oxidative system with refilling phosphocreatine in pauses (Davis: ~70 kJ), so a bout can be mostly aerobic by energy yet decided by glycolytic/alactic bursts. Seluyanov [S5] and Sultanakhmedov [S3] say in words that aerobic capacity governs acidosis and recovery between bursts.
- Davis 2014 [S35] (3 × 2-min semicontact bout vs pads, n = 10, ~70 kg): 77 % aerobic, 19 % PCr, 4 % lactate. The ISSN text cites it as "three 3-min rounds, 86/10/4"; 86/10/4 is the split before moving the ~70 kJ to PCr resynthesis, and the study was 3 × 2 min [inference: ISSN misquotes it; I read only the abstract].
- What changes [inference]: for 3×3 boxing the evidence favours an aerobic base plus repeated alactic bursts; real bouts are "slightly more anaerobic" than the pad bout (authors, as summarised by ISSN). For MMA beyond 4-5 min the aerobic share is higher, yet takedown/scramble phases are glycolytic spikes (a 6-min no-gi sparring study measured only anaerobic energy: ~28 % PCr, ~72 % the rest). Shiyan's 20-60 % glycolytic share is for highly qualified wrestlers in a 100-140-day macrocycle, not a novice's 8 weeks. ISSN: fight camps are "eight-to-ten-week periods … two to three daily training sessions"; off-camp work targets aerobic capacity.

**A3. Blocks in Russian wrestling [S36]:** Menshikov 2012, Greco-Roman, quoting Verkhoshansky's critique of "equal distribution" periodisation. Experimental n = 22 (MS/MSIC) vs control n = 30; one 8-10-session strength block in each of three macrocycles; heavy-load sessions ratchet (none, 3-4, 6-10). Explosive-strength index +20.8 % (55-60 kg) and +22.0 % (66-74 kg) vs +5.8 % and +9 % in controls; p < 0.05 first vs twelfth mesocycle. Non-randomised; the author is a coach; one test [practitioner, journal article].

**A4. Platonov's own text:** not reached. The sibsport PDF of his 2022 "Двигательные качества…" is contents only (chapters «Развитие гибкости в программах тренировочных дней и занятий», «Устойчивость к утомлению») [S38].
