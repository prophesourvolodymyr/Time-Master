## Post 2: DeUpPSGRYWC (matthew.shields7500, "sports performance specialist")
An ad for his paid 12-week "CNS / nervous-system" program ("comment REBUILD for week one"). Claim: movement is a kinetic chain (feet, ankles, knees, hips, core), most people train single muscles, so train the whole chain together, starting at the feet. Footage: barefoot single-leg work on a slant board, then a diagram. No sets, no drills named.
- The chain idea is real (leg and hip power tracks punch force, see camp-research-boxing.md). "Start at the feet" and "CNS training" are program choices and marketing; no controlled trial of such a program in boxers was found. Same family as the Marinovich "nervous system training" note already in the library (no controlled trials in fighters).
- Coordination for a boxer is mostly the sport itself: footwork and shadow rounds, wrestling stance and motion, handstand balance, plyos. Low-risk addition: feet and balance 3 min in the morning flow on speed days (single-leg stand, calf raises, ankle rocks); foot core work has a rationale and small data (see the library note on feet and fascia training).

## Post 3: DfqVqzXyYa9 (yeonnam_pilates, a class demo at a sports medicine school)
"Fascial training with a gym ball": lying back over a stability ball holding two small weights, repeated compression and rebound for the spine (extension/flexion, rotation, lateral flexion) and shoulders (flexion/extension, adduction/abduction). Claim: uses the viscoelastic and elastic properties of muscle and fascia.
- No study of this drill. The theory is Schleip and Muller 2013 (J Bodyw Mov Ther 17:103-115), principles and suggested applications, which the library already files as opinion.
- Fascia work in combat athletes: one small trial, 32 taekwondo players, 8 weeks of manual fascial therapy by physiotherapists (not ball work): reported 20 m sprint -0.36 s vs 0.00, jump +5.75 cm vs +1.88, long jump +9.13 cm vs -0.31 (Sports Med Health Sci 2023, PMID 38314051) [PR]. Large, from a small trial; treat with care.
- Fascia-targeted program in ankle instability: 66 randomized, 43 analyzed, 12 weeks, better ankle scores and jump than a passive control, but 22 of 44 dropped out of the training group against 1 of 22, 3 moderate adverse events, authors include the program's creator (Sports (Basel) 2026, PMID 42931605) [PR].
- What has boxer data: dynamic warm-ups beat static for grip and flexibility, no difference for sprint, jump or reaction time (12 amateur boxers, randomized crossover; J ROL Sport Sci 2025;6(3):569-588) [PR]. Upper-body warm-up review: 31 trials, 628 participants; strong evidence that high-load dynamic warm-ups raise power and strength, short static stretching does nothing for power, no injury-prevention trials (McCrary 2015, BJSM 49:935-942) [PR].
- Closest boxer evidence for "elastic" priming: 10 elite amateur boxers did isometric punches or elastic-band punches before a simulated bout; both gave small worthwhile punch force gains against control; the isometric one was best for 6 of 10, the band for 1 (Finlay 2024, J Strength Cond Res 38(6):1063-1071, PMID 38349345) [PR].
- Correction (user pushback, same day): the first version of this note called the position risky and said skip. That risk line was a guess, and the verdict judged the drill by its fascia story without checking what the movement does. No injury data on this drill was found; nothing here shows it is dangerous. What the drill does: dynamic upper-back extension and rotation plus full shoulder range with light weights over a ball [INF from the on-screen move names: spine extension/flexion, shoulder extension/flexion, shoulder adduction/abduction, spine rotation, lateral flexion].
- Why boxers want that range: Boxing Science (UK boxing S&C company, practitioner source, own testing, not peer-reviewed) says the guard rounds the upper back and shrugs the shoulders, 66% of boxers they test lack shoulder range, improving thoracic extension is a key fix, and their pre-boxing warm-up is dynamic shoulder and thoracic mobility (eagles, windmills, floor slides, Spider-Man with twist) (boxingscience.co.uk/protecting-the-boxers-shoulder). The same range (overhead shoulders plus upper-back extension) is what handstand and press need [INF].
- Evidence for the effect: thoracic extension exercise, alone or with joint mobilization, improved thoracic alignment, shoulder range and function in 30 patients with subacromial impingement over 4 weeks (Healthcare 2020, PMID 32887287) [PR; patients, not boxers]. Stretching review: stretching inside a warm-up with dynamic activity after it is recommended for range of motion with inconsequential effects on performance; dynamic stretching gave small gains in performance when done within minutes of activity; static stretching of 60 s or more lowered it by about 4.6% (Behm 2016, Appl Physiol Nutr Metab, PMID 26642915) [PR].
- Source of the class: the post tags Prof. Hong Jung-gi, dean of the Graduate School of Sports Medicine at CHA University (PhD Oregon State; sports medicine and strength and conditioning), and is captioned as a sports medicine graduate school class. Likely the man teaching; not confirmed.
- Still unproven: the fascia and elastic-bounce explanation, and any punch gain from this exact drill. Stability balls are used for boxers (core and balance work, for example FightCamp), but no named boxer doing this exact drill was found.
- Decision (user, 2026-11-18): keep it as a 4 minute ball warm-up before boxing (Mon, Sat), 5 moves of 8 reps with light weights (his 3 lb dumbbells), small range first, feet wide, stop if the shoulder pinches or the low back complains [INF]. Needs a stability ball (about 65 cm). Priming (2x5 s isometric or banded punches before rounds) stays optional.
