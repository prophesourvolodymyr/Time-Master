# Audit of the research under the 8-week camp (agent ResearchAudit)

**Method.** I read research_science/boxing/wrestling.md, DESIGN.md, BRIEF.md and the library's "Train Tired or Rested?" note. I then opened the cited sources myself: PubMed abstracts, PMC full texts, coach/publisher pages, PDFs. Quotes are copied from what I read. Tags: [peer-reviewed] [practitioner] [lore] [inference]. Verdicts: VERIFIED / OVERSTATED / MISREAD / NOT SUPPORTED / SOURCE UNREACHABLE. Of the ~25 peer-reviewed sources I opened, the quoted numbers matched in all but a handful (listed in section 6). The damage is not in transcription. It is in what was promoted to a rule, what was not carried into the plan, and what is missing.

## 3. What the plan stands on
**Firmly:** (1) a two-week taper that cuts volume ~40-60% and keeps intensity/frequency; (2) no ≥60 s static stretching right before maximal work, and strength before endurance; (3) maximal power peaks late afternoon/evening, so an evening fight is rehearsed in the evening; (4) a beginner needs coach-run, staged sparring; (5) a clean one-arm handstand in 8 weeks is unrealistic and wrist pain is common; (6) strength work, not stretching, lowers injury risk (Lauersen 2014: strength RR 0.315; stretching RR 0.963, CI 0.846-1.095).
**Weakly:** (1) every week-to-week percentage and the 20% cap (Check D); (2) the sparring rules (72 h, 10-14 d, rounds/week); (3) "fresh first" extended to skill and fight-pace rounds, and AM = neural against the circadian data; (4) handstand frequency; (5) plyo/sprint contact caps; (6) the "mostly aerobic" framing (Checks A-B); (7) "he can fight in 8 weeks" (research says 18-30 months; solo boxing; MMA assumed); (8) wrestling content: US/Western YouTube and clinic coaching, no dose evidence, no Russian source (Shiyan 2019 and Russian doctrine are in Checks B-C).

## 9. Targeted checks (run after the first draft)

**A. ISSN 2025: what stands behind ">4 min, >70% aerobic".** Full text (PMC11894756) plus primary abstracts.
- ">4 min" is one karate study: Beneke 2004, 10 nationally/internationally ranked men, 36 fights, mean 267 (61) s with referee stops: aerobic 77.8 (5.8)%.
- Judo, Julio 2017: 12 judoka (18 ± 1 y), 1-5 min simulated matches: "The oxidative system's contribution (70%)", rising "from 50% to 81%" across a match.
- Boxing, Davis 2014: 10 boxers, 3 × 2-min semicontact bout against hand-held pads; authors: "77% Waer, 19% WPCr, and 4% W[lactate]". ISSN prints 86/10/4 and "each lasting 3 min". 86/10/4 equals 526/58/26 kJ without the ~70 kJ of PCr resynthesis the authors add back [inference arithmetic].
- Taekwondo 66 ± 6% (via ISSN, not opened): below 70%.
- Wrestling, via ISSN: Mirzaei 2017 (T&F, HTTP 403, not opened): "glycolytic/lactic acid system contributed the most"; Kraemer: lactate "up to 20 mmol/L"; "determining the precise contribution... remains challenging". BJJ, Pessoa Filho 2021 (10 advanced grapplers): aerobic not measured. MMA: Del Vecchio 2011 (52 athletes) is time-motion only (effort:pause 1:2 to 1:4).
- No study used novices. **Still OVERSTATED:** supported for semicontact boxing, judo, karate; unmeasured for wrestling, BJJ, MMA; the stand's own wrestling text points the other way.

**B. Shiyan 2019 against ISSN** (paper opened: academy.uww.org PDF). Victor V. Shiyan, Russian State University of Physical Education, Moscow; Review Paper, Int J Wrestling Science 9:1, 7-18. Quotes: "14.5% - the effect of alactic anaerobic abilities... 78.2% - glycolytic anaerobic abilities... 7.3% - aerobic capacity"; Fox & Mathews (1981): "90% - anaerobic... 10% - mixed... 0% - aerobic capacity". But his figures come from work "devoted to the study of the contribution of individual metabolic functions to the level of special endurance" (his 1998/2012 studies): which abilities explain a performance score, not ATP supply. No n, method or table is shown, the data are his own, and the paper argues for more anaerobic training. So the two sources mostly answer different questions: share of energy (Beneke, Davis, Julio) versus what limits performance (Shiyan, Chaabene 2017).
- **Better supported.** Boxing: aerobic-majority energy (Davis; El-Ashker >90% VO2max), in experienced boxers' simulated bouts. Wrestling: energy share unsettled (nearest direct accounting, judo, is 70% oxidative) but anaerobic capacity separates winners; Shiyan reports lactate "up to 25-30 mmol/L". MMA: no direct measure.
- **Camp consequence [inference].** ">70% aerobic" does not mean "a zone-2 base is enough". Keep aerobic work as the recovery engine; for the grappling half add repeated maximal efforts with short rests (Shiyan's examples: 3-4 × 400 m with 3-2-1 min rests; series of 15 maximal dummy throws plus 1 min against a fresh partner). For boxing keep round-format aerobic work and alactic speed; glycolytic work is 4% of Davis's energy (Boxing Canada: speed-strength "very high", aerobic stamina "moderate to high").

**C. Russian-language sources on fatigue and microcycles.** Shiyan: "Each training session is dedicated to solving one priority task. All other training tasks are background"; he chose "unidirectional" sessions because combining effects in one lesson has "insufficient theoretical and experimental elaboration". "After three training days with any combination of training loads conducted in a row, there is a significant background of incomplete recovery" (his week: 3 days, rest, 2 days, sauna/rest, two sessions a day). Alactic work goes "at the very beginning of the microcycle"; glycolytic work "is reduced if... done after an aerobic training session". Optimal load "approximately 600-650 minutes" a week of actual work, rest excluded, in 15-20-week macrocycles of highly qualified wrestlers. Against the library note: Shiyan says technique practised "against the background of acute physical fatigue" improves execution under match fatigue (his 1998 data; not verifiable). That is a narrow case for tired practice (performance under fatigue), not for growth. A Belarusian university chapter (ebooks.grsu.by) says to stretch "in the second half of the main part" as a separate block and to use dynamic stretching before speed-strength work; nothing on fatigue improving flexibility. Boxing Canada: skills are consolidated "even when tired" late in a session.

**D. Weekly-% rules.** Buist 2008 RCT (n = 532 novice runners; programme "based on the 10% training rule"): injuries 20.8% vs 20.3%, "no effect". Nielsen 2014 cohort (n = 874 novice runners; <10%, 10-30%, >30% weekly progression): "no statistically significant differences in injury rates"; >30% vs <10% for distance-related injuries HR 1.59 (0.96-2.66), p = .07, "exploratory". Running only; nothing tests 20% or 10-15% in combat or calisthenics. Keep "avoid spikes"; drop the exact numbers as rules.

**E. DESIGN.md week vs "two hard evenings 72 h apart".** The table marks Mon, Wed, Fri, Sat as High (4 of 6 days). Hard-looking evenings: Wed (sparring/hard bag + conditioning), Fri (live wrestling + conditioning), Sat (gym heavy): Wed→Fri 48 h, Fri→Sat 24 h. The science note's own "hard but recoverable" rule (≤2 sessions a week at RPE ≥ 8; each hard day followed by an easy day) is not met [inference; DESIGN is a draft].
