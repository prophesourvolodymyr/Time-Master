# F29-A-c — Eligibility and Consent

## Purpose and Status

One native form lets a signed-in person understand Physique Duels, make private adult and attire declarations, accept the rules, and separately grant AI/safety processing and paired-opponent still sharing. It owns first entry and changed-policy reconsent. [F29-A-w Privacy and sharing](../F29-A-w-privacy-and-sharing/DOCKS.md) owns ongoing choice management, withdrawal, image removal, listing, and request receipts; it may invoke this same grant/reconsent form without creating another settings product.

This is product context and a future implementation specification, not an implemented view, accepted prototype, verified age gate, service-readiness claim, or permission to begin production work. The approved direction covers native iPhone and native macOS, minimal but sufficient text, and the shared vertical sports composition. Numerical and operational proposals in the parent areas retain their stated review status.

[The page-and-match-flow parent](../DOCKS.md) owns navigation and the surrounding journey. [F29-E](../../F29-E-privacy-and-service-operation/DOCKS.md) owns consent, recipients, retention, withdrawal, and deletion authority. This child owns their presentation and interaction here, not a new privacy policy or a different match contract.

## What This View Owns

- First-time eligibility, attire, rule acceptance, and the two distinct sharing choices.
- A concise, readable overview with material privacy facts visible before an affirmative action, and full information deliberately accessible within the same view.
- A truthful distinction between a local selection, a request being saved, and an account choice acknowledged by the service.
- Current-choice inspection, materially changed policy review, decline, private ineligibility, and unavailable or outdated disclosure states.
- Immediate protection and a handoff to the canonical F29-A-w privacy controls for withdrawal, using the same real account, match, and request authority rather than another implementation.
- Equivalent touch, pointer, keyboard, screen-reader, reduced-motion, and energy-conscious behavior on both platforms.

Account authentication, camera setup, policy authorship, legal eligibility decisions, the shared active-match timer, forfeit confirmation, safety-case intake, account deletion, public listing, and the complete battle stage remain with their existing owners. Links reach those real destinations; this view does not recreate them as extra custom screens.

## Chosen Composition and Tradeoffs

Use a single vertical decision sheet in the visual sense, presented as page content for normal entry and as a native attached sheet only when returning from an existing match. It is not a step-by-step wizard, a card carousel, an operating-system permission imitation, or a full-screen legal document with one broad Accept button.

The hierarchy is one Inter Black heading, a brief sports overview, private eligibility and attire, competition rules, AI and safety processing, opponent still access, then the action area. Existing near-black and dark elevated Tmax surfaces carry white readable text and orange interaction accents. Native checkbox and radio semantics remain recognizable. A compact lock symbol identifies private choices but never implies verified age, anonymity, end-to-end encryption, or guaranteed safety.

Keeping every decision on one scrollable surface makes the recipients and consequences comparable without backtracking between steps. The tradeoff is more vertical content than a marketing-style onboarding screen. Readability and informed choice take precedence over fitting everything above the fold. Secondary details expand inline instead of opening a chain of nested sheets or a separate custom legal reader.

This view has no athlete photo, decorative camera preview, opponent, badge award, rating reward, progress ring, consent-completion percentage, ambient celebration, or fake timer. The screenshot reference informs direct hierarchy and a strong primary action, not its people, side-by-side panels, overlaid badges, purple/brown pills, or web styling. Omitting a battle here avoids implying that a stranger is already waiting or that consent must be rushed.

### Relationship to the battle composition

There is no battle stage before real pairing, so initial consent does not manufacture one. If an actual battle remains visible outside a privacy sheet, its order stays real timer first, complete owner-grouped public identity/badge rails next, then two large vertically stacked players, You above Opponent, at every width. Every public badge remains represented; no three-slot cap, hidden count, ticker, clipping, or badge overlay on evidence is introduced. The stage scrolls rather than shrinking the players. No wide Mac arrangement changes the participants into columns.

When opening this view from an active match, capture stops and sensitive stage content is shielded before presentation. The sheet carries the surrounding flow's real stage and remaining-time context at its top and keeps a safe exit action reachable. It does not create a second clock or restart a deadline. A covered stage is not duplicated as miniature players inside the consent form. If the full stage is still visible on a large display, privacy presentation does not compress it into an unreadable side strip.

## Focus, Accessibility, and Input

Reading order follows the visual document: heading, overview, adult requirement, attire, rules, AI/safety, opponent access, action area. Expanded information sits immediately after its invoking row in both visual and accessibility order. Control labels include the recipient/scope, selected or unselected state, current versus draft status, and required-for-this-mode consequence without repeating entire paragraphs on every focus movement.

On first entry, move accessibility focus to the main heading. On a requested submission error, announce the error and focus the relevant summary or unresolved group once. Passive refresh and an opened disclosure do not steal focus from reading. Return from rules/contact, a framework sheet, or a confirmation restores the invoking control or the nearest valid successor if that control no longer exists.

Native roles are preserved: mutually exclusive attire/eligibility choices are radio-like groups, independent legal choices are checkboxes/toggles, disclosure and withdrawal actions are buttons, and policy/contact links are links. No generic tappable rectangle replaces semantic selection. Voice Control names match visible labels. Switch Control can reach every choice, detail, decline, retry, and safe exit without timed gestures or hover.

Text wraps and scales; no Dynamic Type clamp, multi-line legal ellipsis, shrink-to-fit body text, or compulsory horizontal scroll. Selection, restriction, changed policy, and failure use readable text and symbols as well as color. Increased Contrast and Reduce Transparency retain visible focus and opaque, readable form/footer/privacy-cover surfaces. Do not copy low-contrast secondary styling where it makes consequential text unreadable.

Announce a saved choice, policy-change requirement, acknowledged withdrawal, or failure once. Do not repeatedly announce a loading spinner, every remaining-second tick, a selected marker animation, or private declaration text into a notification. There is no haptic or sound reward for consent. A silent, nonanimated path carries exactly the same information.
